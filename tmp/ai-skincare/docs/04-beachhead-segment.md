# Beachhead Segment Analysis

**Product:** AI skin scan → personalized routine → routine-level cheapest-alternative optimization.
**Framework:** Moore's beachhead criteria — burning pain, willingness to pay, winnable share, referral potential.
**Date:** July 2026

---

## 1. Candidate segments (scored 1–5 per criterion)

| Segment | Burning pain | WTP | Winnable | Referral | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Metro dupe-culture skintellectuals** (India, women 18–30, metro/tier-1, active dupe researchers) | 5 | 4 | 4 | 5 | **18** |
| B. r/IndianSkincareAddicts power researchers | 5 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 15 |
| C. Overwhelmed beginners ("just tell me what to buy") | 3 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 10 |
| D. Tier-2/3 value seekers (vernacular-first, price-led) | 4 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 13 |

**Scoring notes:**
- **B** overlaps heavily with A but skews more skeptical and DIY-proud — they built the manual workflow we're automating and are the *hardest* to convert to paid (free-tool loyalty, chemist-level scrutiny). Better used as credibility validators than first customers.
- **C** has the largest TAM in India but diffuse pain (no anchor product to dupe, no price outrage), weak word-of-mouth — and it's where Cureskin already dominates with derm-guided kits. Chasm segment — expand here later.
- **D** has the sharpest price pain of all but needs vernacular content ops, has the lowest subscription WTP, and skews COD/UPI-only with thin affiliate attribution. Strong expansion segment (and Cureskin proves it's reachable), wrong first segment for a solo founder building in English on Instagram.

## 2. Beachhead recommendation

**Primary beachhead — get absurdly specific:**

> **Indian women 18–28 in metro/tier-1 cities who follow 3+ skincare creators on Instagram/YouTube, run a 4+ product routine costing ₹2,000+/month, and have bought or researched at least one dupe in the past 6 months.**

Rough sizing: India has 100M+ online beauty shoppers and urban women 18–28 who buy actives-led skincare number in the several millions; this filtered core is ~800k–2M people — small enough to dominate via one channel (Instagram), large enough that 3–5% paid conversion at ₹499/yr supports a real indie business (~₹1.5–5 Cr ARR ceiling on the beachhead alone).

### Why they win on all four criteria

1. **Burning pain:** they feel routine cost *monthly* and voice it constantly ("is the Estée Lauder serum worth it or is the Minimalist one the same?"). They currently juggle SkinSort + INCIDecoder + Reels comments + Reddit threads — a fragile, hour-long manual workflow per product, made worse because the global tools miss Indian brands and ₹ prices. The pain is recurring (every repurchase, every viral product launch, every Nykaa sale).
2. **Willingness to pay:** they already spend ₹2,000–5,000/mo on product, so ₹499/**yr** is under 1.5% of their annual routine spend — trivially justified if the app saves them one ₹500 swap. Critically, they pay for *savings*, which self-finances: the subscription is framed as "pays for itself on your first swap."
3. **Winnable:** no competitor serves them with neutral personalization (SkinSort/Skinskool are generic and US-centric; Lóvi is expensive, price-blind, and absent from India; Cureskin only sells its own kits). They're concentrated in one channel (Instagram/YouTube) reachable organically. A focused solo builder can plausibly become "the dupe app" for this niche in 12–18 months.
4. **Referral:** dupe culture is *structurally* about sharing finds — the "₹12,000 → ₹3,500 receipt" is exactly the content this segment already posts and forwards on WhatsApp. Screenshots are the referral mechanism; no incentive program needed at first.

### Persona: "Maya, 24" — Bengaluru
Early-career analyst, ~₹6 LPA. 6-step routine (~₹3,000/mo replenishment) mixing Minimalist/The Derma Co actives with one or two K-beauty and prestige splurges from Nykaa. Follows derm-creators and dupe accounts on Instagram/YouTube, lurks r/IndianSkincareAddicts, has SkinSort bookmarked (and grumbles it misses Indian brands), patch-tests, reads INCI lists in the Nykaa app before every sale. Frustration: "I *know* I'm overpaying, but I'm scared the cheap one will break me out." Trigger moments: repurchase time, Nykaa sale FOMO, viral Reels products, empties videos. Success for her: same skin results, visibly lower Nykaa bill, receipts to post.

## 3. Entry wedge and message

- **Wedge use case:** "Show me the cheap twin of *this specific product* — for *my* skin." (Not the full routine builder — that's the upsell after trust is earned on one swap.)
- **Message:** "Scan your face once. Never overpay for skincare again." / proof artifact: the routine receipt.
- **Not the message:** medical-grade analysis claims (invites Cureskin/Lóvi comparison, chemist attacks, and Indian medical-advertising exposure); "AI dermatologist" framing.

## 4. 90-day beachhead acquisition plan

**Days 1–30 — presence before product (runs the validation tests from docs/03):**
- Founder Instagram Reels + YouTube Shorts account: 3 posts/week of manual scan-and-save breakdowns of viral products ("this week's viral K-beauty serum vs. its ₹349 Indian twin — ingredient by ingredient").
- Landing page + waitlist keyed to the receipt hook; capture routine cost + current products at signup (doubles as concierge-MVP intake); WhatsApp community for the waitlist (India's default group channel).
- r/IndianSkincareAddicts methodology post via chemist advisor — credibility, not acquisition.

**Days 31–60 — concierge MVP:**
- Hand-optimize routines for 20–30 waitlist volunteers; publish (with consent) before/after receipts as content.
- Measure: swap acceptance >30%, waitlist referral rate, which product categories drive requests (seeds the database priority list).

**Days 61–90 — first product loop:**
- Ship the MVP loop (face scan + quiz → routine → dupe lookup → daily journal) to waitlist only — scan included as the viral hook, with DPDP-compliant consent-first capture, immediate raw-image deletion, 18+ age gate, and legal review as a launch blocker (E2/E3 mitigations per `docs/05`).
- 10 programmatic SEO pages: "[viral product] dupe for [skin type] India."
- Gate check (from docs/03): waitlist conversion, swap acceptance, scoring credibility all green → build the scan + full optimizer.

## 5. Expansion roadmap (post 60%+ beachhead mindshare)

1. **Adjacent 1 — beginners (segment C):** the beachhead's receipts *are* the marketing that reaches beginners; ship "starter routine under ₹1,500" as the beginner-mode entry.
2. **Adjacent 2 — men's skincare / 30+ anti-aging:** same engine, new content skins; the 30+ "is prestige worth it?" segment has higher WTP.
3. **Adjacent 3 — tier-2/3 + vernacular (segment D):** Hindi-first content and UI, budget-brand depth (drugstore + D2C), COD-friendly retailer links; Cureskin proves the reach is real. This is the mass-India play once the engine and catalog are proven in English metros.
4. **Adjacent 4 — haircare/body care dupes:** hair oils/serums are a massive Indian category; Skinskool already indexes haircare; engine generalizes.
5. **Adjacent 5 — international (SEA/GCC):** the India playbook (local catalog + local prices + dupe culture) exports later; not before national depth.

**Reference flow:** dupe-culture receipts → beginners see them → beginners onboard with quiz → 30+ segment arrives via "worth it or dupe it" content. Each segment's content is the next segment's acquisition.
